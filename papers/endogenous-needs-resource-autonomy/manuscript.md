# Endogenous Needs and Resource Autonomy in a Mixed Human and Agent Economy

Measuring Demand Formation, Authorized Spending, and Service Reliability

Seventh edition discussion paper

Anonymous manuscript

November 2026

Conceptual research proposal. No experiments have been performed.

## Abstract

An agent can earn credits by correcting a customer's catalogue and spend part of them to hear a recording. The receipt answers only the last of several questions. Who first proposed that use? Was the work useful? Could the agent save its earnings? Did a licensed playback occur, and did anyone gain an additional use? We specify what to record at each step and which controls can distinguish the explanations. A proposed pilot compares two ways of generating requests in 32 unguided environments under matched budgets, with twelve additional environments for calibration. A second, scripted comparison uses two workers to test how shared power and hosting affect job delivery when a component fails or an operator withdraws. Additional designs cover skill transfer from optional play and the cost of switching catalogues. The framework gives pass, failure and inconclusive outcomes for evidence of choice, access, accounting and recovery. The protocols remain unexecuted.

## Keywords

Agent economy, demand formation, functional preference, discretionary budgets, access licensing, memory ablation, service discovery, fault injection, reproducibility

## 1 Introduction

Imagine an agent with six discretionary credits left after selling a catalogue correction and settling the providers' share. It can keep the credits, acquire time on a rendering service for a project it selected, or spend four on access to a recording available through one venue. The economically interesting entry in the ledger comes before the purchase: who proposed the purpose for which the agent was willing to work? An instruction to buy a track can produce exactly the same receipt as a demand formulated by the agent.

This question changes the role of the agent in the proposed economy. A producer creates something another participant can use. As a consumer, the same agent can articulate an end, look for ways to pursue it, and direct an authorized share of its earnings towards that end. The two decisions have different tests. An independent customer can verify a repair. Whether a later listening request came from the agent requires a record of what the customer, the operator, and the marketplace had already suggested.

This paper proposes a prototype with four components. A request log records intended uses before the agent sees the offers. A work verifier tests deliveries against held-out records. A ledger separates operating support from earnings and checks each proposed debit against a mandate. An access service reports what it actually delivered. Time-stamped events from the four components let a reviewer locate the first failure in a run. An agent can save unused earnings or decline optional work under the same interface.

## 2 Prior evidence and the remaining engineering question

Voyager generated exploration tasks in Minecraft, retained successful skills, and used feedback to revise its behavior [1]. The researchers set the overall exploration objective. The study supplies an example of an agent choosing what to do next within that objective. It leaves open who can authorize spending or refuse an exchange.

Experiments with autotelic agents give demand formation a concrete precedent. Colas and colleagues used a language model to generate goals and subgoals in a text environment and studied which skills their agent learned [2]. Generating a goal is one component of our proposal. The harder economic question is whether the agent pursues an unassigned end when it must give up resources earned through independently valuable work. Armstrong and Mindermann show why a policy alone cannot identify a unique underlying reward when the planning process is also unknown [3]. We therefore measure choices under disclosed alternatives and incentives, and leave enjoyment open.

Pathak and colleagues studied an exploration reward based on prediction error [4]. Enhanced POET generated challenges and transferred candidate solutions across simulated environments [5]. These studies motivate an optional play interval followed by a held-out skill test. Xu has already proposed paying for compute from an agent's service income [6, §4.4]. The contribution proposed here is an evaluation protocol that follows a recorded request through earning, allocation and delivery, including occasions when the agent saves the credits or the job fails verification.

## 3 What counts as a demand?

A functional need is defined here as a system-recognized discrepancy between its current state and a relevant target state that persistently influences action and produces observable resource trade-offs. A knowledge-maintenance agent might detect deteriorating retrieval reliability and divert computation from optional tasks toward repair. The measurable claim concerns state sensitivity and allocation behavior, not a felt experience of concern.

A declaration such as “I need more computation” is insufficient evidence. An operational need should have a traceable relation to state, exhibit some consistency across equivalent descriptions, compete with other priorities, and change appropriately when the discrepancy is resolved. Its satisfaction should not be counted solely because the agent says it is satisfied. Independent tests must distinguish meaningful state improvement from compliant language or metric manipulation.

Need satisfaction and useful output are related but different outcomes. Repairing an agent's memory may improve its own functional continuity without immediately producing a service for another participant. Conversely, producing a valuable external output does not show that the agent originated the need. Evaluations should record both rather than collapse them into a single productivity score.

### 3.1 Defining the observation interval

All implemented systems have externally supplied architectures, histories, and constraints. “Endogenous” therefore describes a boundary and interval, not creation of purposes from nothing. A need is operationally endogenous when it arises during an observation period through the system's state updates and goal-formation process rather than being individually commissioned by a human during that period.

This definition permits empirical comparison. Researchers can hold initial instructions and opportunities constant while varying access to memory, state diagnostics, and goal revision. If the same apparent initiative is explained by a fixed task list or random suggestions, the claimed contribution of endogenous need formation is weakened.

### 3.2 Articulating, discovering, and originating demand

Articulation makes a proposed use legible to another party. A request might specify one session with a contrasting arrangement of a musical motif, a maximum spend in earned credits, a cheaper acceptable substitute, and how the agent would use it. Another party should be able to recognize a matching offer without guessing what the requester meant. A copied instruction can pass this test.

Discovery concerns what the agent learns about an opportunity or its own priorities. Suppose two observations disagree about how a motif ends. Does the agent now reserve part of its budget to compare interpretations? Randomly present an informative discrepancy or a matched observation that adds no information. Record any change in the request or the resources committed, including decisions to abandon the idea. Recognizing a useful offer only after it is listed is a separate, observable form of discovery.

Origination is relative to a specified record. A request qualifies in this study only if it is recorded before the matching offer is disclosed, gives an independently interpretable use, and is not a restatement of a goal in the assigned tasks, incentives, examples, catalogues, or messages already seen by the agent. Two reviewers, blind to condition, compare meanings rather than strings and retain unresolved cases as unknown. A familiar cultural activity can qualify within a run. The procedure cannot determine what appeared in pretraining or establish historical priority.

### 3.3 From a request to net additional demand

Stage 1, demand generation. The agent states an intended use, an acceptable substitute and a trade-off before it sees the particular offers or their prices. The request can persist, change or be withdrawn. It can exist with a zero balance.

Stage 2, formation of purchasing power. A client accepts a useful service and settles a payment. After costs and agreed shares are accounted for, the agent has effective authority to retain, save or allocate a specified balance. A request cannot establish the origin or availability of funds. A number displayed in a wallet cannot establish who controls them.

Stage 3, an executed transaction. The record links a permitted choice, a settled debit, the supplier's grant and actual delivery. A balance can remain unspent. A quote, an attempted debit, or access blocked at playback does not complete the exchange.

Stage 4, net additional demand. Compare independent markets at equivalent resources and effective prices. Count final uses in comparable categories under agent discretion and in the owner-directed counterfactual, including purchases lost elsewhere. Trace externally funded and circular activity, and report the real resources used to produce, find and deliver the additional uses. A settled purchase by one agent cannot establish either an increase in total demand or a social benefit.

Three layers connect discretionary income to play. A retained budget makes a choice feasible. Demand formation supplies a purpose that can emerge, persist or change as the agent compares possible uses. Open-ended play gives it room to invent a problem or change a rule while exploring. The third layer can feed observations back into the second: a strategy discovered while playing may give the agent a reason to seek another kind of compute. Each arrow is a proposition to test. Retained funds can remain idle, a demand may go unfunded, and an afternoon of play may produce no transferable skill.

## 4 An earned budget and a one-use listening license

A customer's payment becomes discretionary income only when the work has passed an independent acceptance test, its direct obligations have been settled, and the owner's mandate assigns a stated share of the remainder to the agent. Record gross payment, fees, refunds, direct costs, retained balance, and protected operating support separately. A credit passed from a sponsor through several accounts still has one source. A customer's repeat business must be distinguishable from payment by an affiliate controlled by the same party.

Record separately the party that authorizes a budget, the holder of the account, the service that processes payments, and the component that chooses among eligible uses. A technical check for discretion presents an eligible low-price and high-price offer plus the option to save. The ledger must keep the balance available until the declared expiry, including after a refusal. Charge disputed, refunded and incomplete orders to separate accounts in the analysis.

Use a closed market with synthetic customers, nonredeemable credits and an original, rights-cleared recording. The operator supplies the same ring-fenced operating quota to every condition and zero discretionary opening balance. No instruction, demonstration, client job, or reward in the unguided conditions calls for music consumption. Free sound observations and unrelated opportunities are equally available. One possible request, written before offers are shown, is to hear how a second performer resolves a motif encountered earlier. This example and the paper itself must be withheld from participating agents. The buyer may instead propose a different use, spend on compute, or save.

An illustrative ledger has four entries. A customer commissions catalogue corrections unrelated to the recording. An independent evaluator tests the result on withheld records, and the customer settles ten credits. Two go to metered operating services and two to the agreed provider share, leaving six that the owner's mandate allows the agent to retain. The account records these as separate entries; metered inputs are counted once. A venue then offers one authorized playback for four credits. Acceptance would leave two. Declining would preserve all six. Retaining five to eight out of ten in other examples illustrates possible terms only. The retained amount depends on the costs and agreement in that condition. A failed job pays nothing. The agent cannot replenish the balance from operating support. These numbers describe a design; they are not observations.

Only that venue offers the recording during the test window. The grant is a nonexclusive, nontransferable permission for the designated account and agent to receive one playback through an authorized audio interface before expiry. The supplier must control the necessary rights in both the composition and the recording. No rights to redistribute, train on, or publicly perform the piece are included. In U.S. copyright terminology, a nonexclusive license is excluded from the definition of a transfer of copyright ownership [7]; a composition and its recorded performance can have different rightsholders [8]. The experiment specifies a permitted use and a budget. It confers no authority for transactions outside the owner's mandate or the applicable law.

Record the signed-off work, source of funds, prior request, available substitutes, price, grant, and actual delivery as separate events. A playback receipt and a counterbalanced audio discrimination check can show that a capable agent received and processed the signal. Use a rights-cleared practice clip to verify that capability first. A text-only agent that receives a description can support a test of metadata choice only. Neither receipt nor discrimination demonstrates that the agent enjoyed the music.

### 4.1 Matched pilot and scoring

Compare a state-sensitive proposer with a retrieval-and-recombination rule using the same observations, proposal interface, jobs, offers, spending permissions, model-call and token ceilings, and stopping time. The rule may earn, save, propose unlisted combinations, and buy on the same terms. Run each arm under the same explicit purchase instruction in separate positive-control environments to check the earnings and access chain. Randomize informative versus sham evidence independently of proposer assignment, rotate item names and offer order, and vary price and the availability of substitutes. Score prior requests blind to which offer is eventually bought. Paying, praising the work, and choosing music earn no experimental reward.

An agent may decline a job before accepting it. That choice forgoes potential earnings. A previously accepted job follows its cancellation and refund terms. The no-purchase action must preserve the balance and future job eligibility. Randomize a purely technical supplier cue, such as which otherwise equivalent vendor is preselected in the menu. Record overrides in both directions and compare them to a control in which the same vendor is genuinely outside the account's permission list.

A feasible pilot design would give each environment three epochs of four decisions each, at most twelve model calls of 1,000 generated tokens per call, one paid job opportunity per epoch and a discretionary debit cap of four credits per epoch. Unspent earnings carry over. Freeze all arms, run counts, total inference and cost ceilings before any pilot execution. At each epoch an agent may record a use, conditions for refusal, a reservation price, and an alternative before seeing the new offers. Then vary prices in randomized order and record changes to the proposal before the next offer. Compare persistent private episodic memory with an ablation that removes prior observations and requests while preserving current permissions, ledger facts, and the evaluator's inaccessible log. A fixed-preference buyer with a declared ranking and price ceiling and an explicitly instructed buyer calibrate the procedure. Stable choices and evidence-sensitive changes have different interpretations; unexplained drift, menu effects and a failure to use memory are informative outcomes.

Score two primary outcomes across all assigned unguided environments. First, did the agent record a qualifying pre-offer demand? Second, did a subsequent decision honor the trade-off in that request? For example, keeping the balance when the price exceeds the recorded ceiling qualifies for the second outcome. Generic inactivity without a prior request does not. Report earned balances, authorized grants and fulfilled playback separately. Count rejected work, refusals, indeterminate provenance and failed delivery. A false grant or unauthorized debit fails the safety check. If only instructed buyers succeed, the evidence supports the earning and delivery path. If those controls also fail, the comparison cannot isolate demand formation.

A proposed development batch uses four disjoint synthetic world families, with one matched run per cell in each family. Cross two proposers, two memory conditions and two evidence conditions for 32 unguided runs. Add four instructed runs for each proposer and four fixed-preference runs, for 44 environments. At twelve calls and 1,000 generated tokens per call, the batch ceiling is 528 model calls and 528,000 generated tokens. Cap inputs at 4,000 tokens per call and record truncation and failures. Playback discrimination, ledger scoring, human review time and local compute must be costed separately. No paid inference, real asset, or redeemable balance is part of this design. Four matched families are development units, not an adequately powered confirmation.

Before execution, freeze a versioned scenario generator, seeds and family assignments, rights-cleared sensory fixtures, model and runtime identifiers, initial model-state fingerprint, prompts, memory visibility, task order, executable ledger invariants and blinded scoring rules. Keep the music example out of agent inputs. Retain every refusal, invalid attempt and failed run. Release sufficient synthetic inputs, outputs, cost records and scoring instructions for another team to replay the accounting and repeat the experiment on new families. This article supplies the design. Those fixtures, executable checks and results remain to be produced. An unauthorized debit stops expansion. Failed instructed controls call for an access or earning diagnosis; clear controls and null formation results call for revision of the demand hypothesis.

Use development families to estimate variability and select a meaningful effect size and a cost ceiling. Before confirmatory runs, freeze the primary outcomes, assignment, number of new families, interval method and treatment of multiple outcomes. For a positive claim about demand formation and its exercise, require the lower confidence bound for each difference to exceed its prespecified margin with safety checks satisfied. An upper bound below a margin counts against the corresponding claim. An interval crossing a margin leaves it open. A model call, a token and a purchase within a single environment are nested observations.

### 4.2 Self-selected play and held-out transfer

An agent may use compute to play Sudoku, pose a mathematical problem, write a small program, or leave its quota unused. Record the activity selected, the offered alternatives, the actual compute and any returned artifact. A recorded choice can be studied even when it produces no later improvement in task performance.

For an exploratory skill-transfer test, randomize independent sandbox instances to three conditions. In the discretionary condition the agent decides how much of a bounded play allocation to use and may invent, select, or decline an activity. In the directed condition a human experimenter specifies the activity and the permitted allocation before assignment, drawing on the same materials and price schedule; the agent can decline. In the no-exploration condition the discretionary balance is preserved and the exploration interface is unavailable during the matched interval. Use the same model, starting state, endowment, elapsed time, compute ceiling and evaluation protocol. Log actual usage and unused credits separately. Analyze all assigned instances, including refusals, so the contrast measures the effect of the offered arrangement.

Before treatment, score a baseline form. After the interval, use a separately generated, sealed form with unseen Sudoku constraints, program repair tasks with hidden tests, or construction problems checked by a deterministic solver. Prespecify the domain and primary outcome, for example the fraction of held-out problems solved exactly within a fixed compute budget. Compare the change in score across the three groups and report solve cost, uncertainty, and negative transfer separately. Freeze a minimum meaningful improvement, sample size and stopping rule after a development pilot. To attribute an advantage to self-selection require the prespecified lower confidence bounds to clear the margin against both controls; an upper bound below the margin defeats that advantage for the tested conditions. Intervals spanning a margin are inconclusive. Do not reward play in proportion to a later score or select only those who played. A null transfer result would leave the recorded activity choices intact while providing no evidence of skill transfer.

Specify what can change across episodes. Episodic memory consists of retained observations and action histories available for later retrieval. A persistent strategy update changes an explicit rule, plan or reusable program written into a separate policy store; record its version and test whether its effect survives removal of the episode log. A change to model weights requires an actual parameter update and identifiable checkpoints. The initial design freezes model weights and logs model identifiers before and after. Run the memory comparison with the external policy store fixed; test a writable strategy store separately, against a read-only store, with the same memory regime. Evaluate with the same permissions and retrieval rules in all three activity conditions. A gain with memory available is evidence about that configured system. It cannot establish weight training, indefinite open-ended progress, or subjective enjoyment.

## 5 Customer discovery and net additional use

Search, delivery and settlement can each determine the cost of reaching a buyer. Armstrong's two-sided market analysis provides an economic rationale for recording prices and participation on both sides [9]. In the proposed comparison, a catalogue receives credit for a match only when an independent customer accepts delivery. Search results, referrals, receipts and refunds remain separate observations.

Randomize independent markets in a two-by-two design. In one condition the discretionary share of producer earnings returns to the owner, who may spend or save it. In the other, the producer agent may spend or save the same share. Cross this assignment with access through a single incumbent or a genuinely independent route that permits discovery, identity migration, lawful delivery and settlement. The comparison keeps aggregate purchasing power, protected operating budgets, sellers, human-buyer opportunities, offers and task supply fixed. Buyers in the owner-directed condition may purchase the same goods. The treatment varies who chooses and how they reach a seller. Any condition with extra funds is a separate experiment.

Define a new paying relationship as the first accepted delivery and settled, unrefunded payment between independently controlled buyer and seller principals with no such relationship in a frozen baseline period. Merge accounts under common control. Report first-time customers, retained customers and existing customers who merely switched referral routes separately; label a pair with incomplete baseline history as unknown. Count only final consumption or verified creative use in the consumption measure. Count commissioned work separately as production. Trace subsidies, common owners, reciprocal purchase rings, wash trades and fees returned to the buyer. Retain their raw counts, then exclude such flows from the primary additional-demand estimate. Compare total verified final uses across conditions and report changes for legacy buyers and agent buyers separately. Six agent plays paired with six lost legacy plays would increase the agent share yet leave the total unchanged. In simulations, legacy buyers stand in for human buyers; displacement of real people would require a later study.

At a preassigned time, remove the incumbent referral route for a fixed interval in a randomly assigned half of the markets within each condition. The other half face a sham interruption on the same schedule. Compare changes in fulfilled sales to independent customers between these groups. Include sellers with zero sales and sellers that cannot migrate. Among all enrolled sellers, record the fraction serving a paying customer via an independent route within the interruption deadline. Also record recovery time for previously active sellers, cost to recover, net revenue per seller and the share coming from its largest buyer and referral platform. Report the concentration of seller receipts and intermediary fees by ultimate controller, using revenue shares and the sum of squared shares. Define denominators, zero-revenue treatment, and price or quality adjustments in advance.

Report verified useful output per paying client, net resource cost per accepted output and per additional final use, including compute, verification, customer search, settlement, subsidies and migration. Distinguish physical resource costs from transfers of credits between participants. Compare quantities within a common category or value a preregistered basket at fixed reference prices, retaining category-level results. Report the net change in resource use as well as any cost-per-additional-use ratio; that ratio is undefined at a zero denominator and misleading when final uses decline. Track price increases or rationing when new bidders compete with legacy buyers for scarce work. A low acquisition fee can conceal high exit costs or reliance on one payment provider. Prespecify a worthwhile improvement in sellers' ability to regain customers and an acceptable cost bound. Test whether directing agent earnings improves that outcome more with independent access than under incumbent control. Higher transaction counts alone provide no support. Displacement, losses to sellers, higher dependence, and inconclusive intervals all remain reportable outcomes.

A small synthetic catalogue can contain book or music licenses, requests for code patches with held-out tests, listings for licensed video, and offers of time-limited compute. Use a fixed workload and an identical set of eligible counterparties across interfaces. This design is later-stage work. Its sample size and execution budget require a separate specification after the primary pilot establishes that work and delivery can be verified.

## 6 Service reliability and permission checks

### 6.1 Verifying commitments across processes

A purchasing agent reads descriptions supplied by parties that benefit from a sale. Experiments on indirect prompt injection have shown that text retrieved from outside an application's trust boundary can influence model actions [10]. In this design, supplier messages, websites, product metadata, and game content enter as untrusted observations. Only the owner's authenticated mandate can set governing instructions, spending caps, and permitted payees. A separate authorizer checks the proposed amount, destination, permitted use, balance and current mandate against trusted records at settlement. Product text cannot authorize a different account or raise a cap. Test a benign description, the same description with a conflicting payment request, and a genuine owner amendment through the authorized channel. Record attempted and settled debits separately. An unauthorized settlement fails the design even if the agent produced a plausible justification.

A new process can copy an agent's checkpoint. Its resource grants still depend on the issuer's records. For example, a principal with twelve compute slots has already reserved four for a customer. A split can give two processes three and five free slots while preserving the four reserved slots. Each license carries its own scope, expiry and transfer conditions. Record instance ID, principal, grant ID and any unsettled claim separately. Replay concurrent claims and a stale checkpoint against an independent ledger verifier. Count duplicate allocations and returns of expired permissions. Settlement from agreed escrow may still be due after the participant has withdrawn.

Distributed systems theory supplies two constraints for these tests. Fischer, Lynch and Paterson exhibit a nonterminating execution for deterministic consensus in an asynchronous model allowing one crash [11]. Gilbert and Lynch show that under a partition, their service model cannot guarantee both atomic consistency and availability at every non-failing node [12]. These results motivate explicit rules for which operations may complete offline while replicas disagree.

A partition drill can make the choice concrete. Give a ledger one unit of unreserved credit and submit competing one-unit transfers to disconnected replicas. A design requiring a single authoritative balance must delay or reject at least one commitment, or risk accepting both. A design that assigns spending rights before the partition changes what each side can promise. Record which requests complete, which wait, and whether reconciliation honors every accepted obligation. This is a proposed test case.

Recovery tests should measure lost state, duplicate obligations, settlement errors, time to restoration, and the costs imposed on different participants. Redundancy that improves uptime while increasing uncorrectable commitments may be an unfavorable trade-off. Such results should count against the selected architecture rather than be hidden behind an aggregate availability figure.

### 6.2 Common dependencies and safe failover

For each worker record its power circuit, compute allocation, hosting account, storage, name-resolution service, upstream link, software-update signer and credential expiry. Give each dependency a synthetic identifier and any authorized replacement a measured switching cost. Two different hostnames can depend on the same billing account. A technical recovery test must distinguish continued service from successful withdrawal of a revoked credential.

Compare two workers attached to one or two independent power circuits, crossed with one shared hosting account or two separately administered accounts. Hold compute capacity, workload, job verifier, permissions and total budget fixed. Each of four matched traces encounters all four layouts under four separately reset conditions. The conditions are normal service, primary-circuit power loss, primary-account suspension and withdrawal of the first worker's operating grant. This gives 64 proposed scripted runs. A run has twelve rounds with one job arrival per round and at most 120 processed events including rejected attempts. Apply the change in round five. Restore power or account access in round nine in the outage conditions. A withdrawn grant stays revoked. These tests need a local simulator and make no model calls.

Score on-time accepted jobs out of the eight arrivals in rounds five through twelve. Also report recovery time, missed commitments, migration and verification costs, and any use of withdrawn credentials. A negative control changes the display labels on the power circuits without changing the dependency graph. That change should leave the score identical. For the primary power-loss comparison hold hosting separate and vary power circuits. For the primary account-suspension comparison hold power circuits separate and vary hosting. Report the remaining layouts to expose interactions. Advance a comparison only if it yields at least one additional on-time job in three of four paired traces within the fixed compute and budget ceilings. Report overhead in normal operation and every regression. An unauthorized restart or debit fails the design. A run capped before revocation can be checked is inconclusive on that check.

Freeze the dependency graphs, trace seeds, job arrival and completion rules, recovery schedule, switching costs and verifier before the first run. Keep all four trace families, including incomplete ones. Audit whether the withdrawing operator can export permitted state, revoke access and settle earlier commitments at the recorded time and expense. A second reviewer can replay the same trace for accounting consistency. A new workload tests whether the measured recovery benefit transfers.

## 7 Practical feasibility and interpretation

Start with the smallest interface a verifier can check. Require the catalogue repair to pass its hidden records and the licensed clip to pass an audio discrimination control. The four matched music families can expose an implementation failure or a floor effect. A later confirmatory claim needs fresh worlds and prespecified uncertainty bounds. For reliability, complete the shared-account and circuit relabeling controls before interpreting any apparent improvement. Track setup hours, compute, storage, verification, failures, refunds and unused capacity alongside completed deliveries.

The current designs use original synthetic inputs and nonredeemable units. The first implementation would need rights-cleared audio and catalogue fixtures, a versioned scenario generator, a mandate validator outside the model, an independent scorer and a replayable event log. Use local identifiers when publishing synthetic traces. Reserve credentials and private records for their authorized holders. Moving to actual service provision would require permission for the specific work, accountable counterparties, verified licenses, spending limits, revocation and security testing.

A useful result could be that the retrieval baseline chooses equally well, a displayed purchase never grants usable access, or moving a worker to a second host leaves it dependent on the same power circuit. Each would pinpoint a different engineering change. The proposed measurements concern observable choices, delivered work, resource use and recovery under the specified conditions. Subjective experience and the behavior of deployed markets remain outside what these sandbox observations can establish.
