## Novelty discipline

The manuscript uses “we propose,” “we formalize,” and “we investigate.” It does not claim to be the first neutrino communication framework. The 2012 experiment and earlier communication proposals are explicit prior art. Any narrower novelty claim is conditional on the search documented here and requires human bibliographic review.

### Reproducible title and concept search

The search was executed on 2026-08-27 with database publication filters ending 2026-08-26. The following literal queries were checked: the complete working title normalized to ASCII punctuation; `When One Bit Is Enough`; `probabilistic neutrino trigger channel`; `neutrino communication high-frequency trading`; and `One Bit Is Enough: a Framework for`. Crossref REST `query.bibliographic` searches returned and screened the first 100 ranked records per query; OpenAlex title searches used `to_publication_date:2026-08-26`; exact-title web search and ResearchHub title search were used as supplementary discovery routes. Ranked-result limits mean this is a bounded search, not proof of universal absence.

No exact complete-title match or exact `probabilistic neutrino trigger channel` title match was found. Vasic, Kuntimaddi, and Kostic, “One bit is enough: A framework for deploying explicit feedback congestion control protocols,” doi:10.1109/COMSNETS.2009.4808893, is an unrelated congestion-control title sharing the lead phrase and is not a serious full-title collision. A dated 2012 public discussion of neutrinos and trading was screened as non-academic evidence of prior public ideation, not as support for any technical claim. Two adjacent 2026 exotic/low-rate communication records were also screened; neither supplied this paper's hybrid fallback and deadline-economic model, and neither is used as evidence. The full working title is retained, while the paper makes no claim to first conception of neutrino trading, one-bit communication, or low-rate neutrino links.
